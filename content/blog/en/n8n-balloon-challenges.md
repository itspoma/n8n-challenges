n8n Balloon Challenges is a hands-on event format for people who want to practice automation together. You choose a challenge, build a working workflow, explain it to a mentor, and collect a balloon when your solution is approved. Each balloon is a small, visible celebration of something you have learned.

The format was created by Roman Rodomansky as his own approach to bringing practical n8n learning and knowledge sharing into the same room.

## Learn by building something that works

Watching a demonstration can help you understand an idea. Building a workflow gives you a chance to try it yourself, run into questions, and work through them with other people.

The challenges give that practice a clear starting point. Pick a mission that fits your team, read the brief, and start building. You do not have to complete every challenge or follow a fixed order.

- Choose a challenge that interests your team.
- Build and run the workflow in n8n.
- Show a mentor how your solution works.
- Collect the challenge balloon after approval.
- Share a useful discovery with someone else.

The balloon color identifies the challenge, rather than your score. The aim is to make progress visible and give people a reason to ask each other what they have built.

## Sharing knowledge is part of the challenge

A working workflow is only part of the experience. Explaining your choices helps you understand them more clearly, and another participant may notice an approach you have not considered.

Ask questions, compare solutions, and talk through the parts that were difficult. Someone who has just solved a problem can often help the next person get past the same obstacle. Mentors support that process by listening, asking questions, and checking the result.

## Inspired by NodeSchool

The inspiration comes from [NodeSchool](https://nodeschool.io/), which offers self-guided programming exercises. The idea of learning through practical exercises, with room to explore at your own pace, is a natural starting point for a community workshop.

Roman adapted that inspiration into an original event format centered on n8n workflows, team participation, mentor check-ins, and collectible balloons. It brings its own playful identity to the experience of learning by doing.

## A format designed to grow

The format is designed to scale from a small group to a larger workshop. Teams can work on different challenges at the same time, while a shared set of briefs gives everyone a clear task to follow.

Growing the event still means planning enough mentor support, workspace, and time for people to demonstrate their solutions. The challenge structure can be reused while organizers adjust the selection and support to suit their group.

New challenges can also extend the experience as participants develop their skills. The basic rhythm stays simple: choose, build, explain, and share.

## Bring your curiosity

You do not need to leave with every balloon. One working workflow, a clearer understanding of a tricky idea, or a useful conversation can make the session worthwhile.

[Explore the challenges](https://n8n-challenges.app/en) and find something you would like to build. Bring your questions, help someone when you can, and let the balloons tell the story of what your team learned.
